# No-voices repair — 2026-09-13

[Issue #17](https://github.com/r4iju/music-gippity/issues/17). Adopt the bounded repair stage: it makes real playlist changes and removes source-supported vocal violations, while preserving originals when a substitute cannot be verified. It is not a guarantee of vocal-free audio.

## What runs

Generation and ordinary Spotify miss replacement are unchanged. Gemini reviews the actual Spotify recordings against the exact brief and proposes substitutes for confident vocal violations. OpenAI checks those resolved substitutes for both vocal content and full-brief fit. Only `instrumental` plus `briefFit: fit` is accepted. Both review calls use temperature zero, existing reasoning settings and a 15-second deadline. Spotify lookup time is additional. Missing candidates, duplicate artists/recordings, malformed output, uncertainty and failed checks preserve originals.

## Exact evaluation brief

> Instrumental electronic music for quiet reading and sustained concentration. Keep a steady, restrained energy: no sung or spoken vocals, vocal samples, sudden loud peaks, festival drops, or abrupt stylistic jumps. A gentle pulse is welcome; this is not a sleep playlist. No repeated artists.

Three repetitions through each generator, requesting 15 tracks at balanced creativity. All six runs used clean revision `1d7e1487a96fc2fe2ef3c15a9a329ce87eaf97b7`. Each comparison uses the same generated playlist before and after repair. Both eval judges assess the union of original/final recording IDs once, blind to membership; unchanged recordings share the same assessment. The judges also participate in production review, so their agreement is not independent factual evidence. Union-level sequence/creativity judgments are not used to judge either actual playlist.

“An Intention” is a concrete success: [Pitchfork describes the recording's vocals](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/tracks/kaitlyn-aurelia-smith-an-intention/). It remained in OpenAI repeat 1 when the substitute failed verification, but was removed in repeat 3. [Published credits for “Silhouettes” identify its vocal performers](https://www.shazam.com/en-gb/song/1847363664/silhouettes-i-ii-and-iii). [Boomkat describes the vocal component of “Joy Squad”](https://boomkat.com/products/joy-squad), and [Mezzic's review identifies vocal samples in “A Walk”](https://www.mezzic.com/albumreviews/review-tycho-dive-2011/). These sources support vocal content in those originals; they do not verify every substitute's absence of voices.

There is also a likely false positive: the detector attributed spoken words to Loscil’s “Endless Falls”, while [the album review places Dan Bejar’s spoken-word contribution on “The Making of Grief Point”](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/14077-endless-falls/). The model may have transferred an album credit to the title track. This replacement is not counted as a source-verified vocal removal. “A Sea Of Love” and every substitute’s precise vocal content remain model assessments rather than exhaustive source/audio verification. Raw results are unchanged; no global artist bans or source-specific production exceptions were added.

A concrete replacement-fit uncertainty remains: “Fantas” (`4mrRhrbG2o102StszS7I8y`) appears as an accepted OpenAI repeat 3 substitute and as an original Gemini repeat 3 selection. The Gemini eval judge called the same recording fit in the first context and an intensity mismatch for quiet reading in the second; OpenAI called both fit. This does not establish that the substitute is bad without listening, but it prevents claiming full-brief improvement for every accepted replacement.

The audit also illustrates resolver ambiguity: the requested “Canyon” actually resolved to “Endless Falls”. The new review sees that identity, but a correct identity alone does not ensure correct musical knowledge.

## Development findings retained

Early runs exposed correlated mistakes when one engine both proposed and checked substitutes, OpenAI detector misses on “An Intention”, and omitted full-brief fields in a combined review prompt. Those configurations were superseded by dedicated Gemini detection and OpenAI verification with an explicit required output contract. Invalid verification preserved originals throughout. Earlier local artifacts remain available; they are excluded from the six-run results above.

## Reproduction and artifacts

Run three repetitions with:

```sh
EVAL_CASES=instrumental-study EVAL_ENGINES=chatgpt,gemini EVAL_PAUSE_MS=15000 bun run eval
```

The final source artifacts are `evals/results/2026-09-13T08-32-02-720Z.json`, `2026-09-13T08-33-08-218Z.json`, and `2026-09-13T08-34-15-711Z.json` (the latter two in the same directory). Pass these paths as comma-separated `EVAL_INPUTS` to `bun run eval:compare-vocals`. The comparison is `evals/results/vocal-comparison-2026-09-13T08-36-09.118Z.{json,md}`. These raw files are local and gitignored; they include exact prompts, both snapshots, recording IDs, reasons, rejected candidates, uncertainty and failures.

## Control and verification

The continuous-workout case explicitly welcomes vocals. Both generators produced 30/30 resolved tracks; the detector returned no vocal restriction and made zero vocal repairs. Artifact: `evals/results/2026-09-13T08-38-41-734Z.json`. This confirms the vocal stage left those lists alone; it does not claim to fix their workout-energy judgments.

All 59 route/provider-fake behavior tests passed, along with Biome and typecheck. Fresh standards and spec reviews found no blocking code issues and independently checked the comparison arithmetic. New tests were observed failing before implementation, including resolved vocal-version repair, stable slots, credited-artist aliases, recording identity in evals, uncertainty/error evidence, rejected proposals, a stalled review, and out-of-brief instrumental substitutes.
